1.The grid

Five vowels across, consonants down. Read it as a·i·u·e·o and then the same five sounds with each consonant in front. The dashes are real gaps: those syllables do not exist in modern Japanese.

—ん — belongs to no row: no vowel, but still a full beat of its own

Irregular readings: し is shi, ち is chi, つ is tsu, ふ is fu made with both lips and no teeth. を is pronounced o, exactly like お, and is only ever the object particle. Everything else is exactly consonant + vowel.

2.Stroke order

Numbers mark where each stroke begins. Three habits cover almost every character: top before bottom, left before right, horizontal before the vertical that crosses it.

This chart is drawn by the browser and needs JavaScript. With it disabled, use the grid in section 1 for the character set.

The 25 marked characters — が, ざ, だ, ば and ぱ rows — are on their own sheet: tenten and maru.

3.Confusion pairs to watch

These are the shapes that get mixed up, mostly in the first month. Writing both members of a pair side by side fixes them faster than looking at them does.

ThisvsTell them apart by
さ saち chiさ opens to the left, ち opens to the right — mirror images
き kiさ saき has two crossbars, さ has one
ぬ nuめ meぬ finishes with a loop, め does not
ね neれ re / わ waね ends in a full loop, れ in a hook, わ in a curve
つ tsuし shiつ is a horizontal wave, し a vertical hook
に niた taに is upright + two marks, た is a cross + a hook
は haほ ho / ま maほ is は with a second crossbar; ま has no left-hand upright at all
る ruろ roる closes its loop, ろ leaves it open — the only difference
り riい iり leans in and its right stroke runs long; い stands straight and even
を oお osame sound, different job — を is only ever a particle, お is a letter inside words

Two of those rows carry a caveat the words alone do not show. In ほんをよむ, を marks the object and does nothing else — it is never a letter inside a word. And every ん word in the table has ん somewhere other than the front, because ん never begins one.
