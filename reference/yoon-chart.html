Yōon — the small ゃ ゅ ょ

All thirty-three contracted sounds in both scripts, the rule that generates them, the four pairs where size alone decides the word, and forty-five words they unlock — in Japanese, English and Modern Standard Arabic. Made to print.

Taught in lesson 7 (hiragana) and lesson 9 (katakana) · readings from Jisho.org (JMdict) · beat counts after Vance, The Sounds of Japanese

1.The rule, in three lines

  • Only a kana ending in -i takes a small ゃ ゅ ょ. Eleven do: き し ち に ひ み り and ぎ じ び ぴ. Eleven times three is thirty-three.
  • The small kana replaces the i, it does not add a syllable. ki → ky + a = kya.
  • The result is one beat, not two. きゃ is one mora; きや is two.

い is excluded because it is a bare vowel with no consonant to bend. ぢゃ ぢゅ ぢょ exist and are never written, exactly as ぢ is not.

The same thirty-three, in katakana

Identical rule, identical arithmetic — and different holes. Five of the six cells the hiragana chart above could not fill are filled here (ヒュ ミュ ビュ ピュ ピョ), because those sounds arrived in Japanese as loanwords and loanwords are written in katakana.
