Reference sheet

Question words

Thirty-two ways to ask something, built from four stems, plus the こそあど grid that generates six of them and the two answers that arrive with them. Japanese, English and Modern Standard Arabic throughout. Made to print.

Taught in lesson 6 · readings from Jisho.org (JMdict) · sets after Tofugu

1.The four stems

StemAsksBecause
ど
do		 which? There is a set in front of both of you and one member has to be picked out of it.
なに · なん
nani · nan		 what? how many? There is no set. The field is open, or a number is wanted.
だれ
dare		 who? Neither pattern. Its polite partner どなた does belong to a こそあど set.
い
i		 how many? how much? when? Nothing is being identified at all. A number is wanted — いつ a time, いくつ a count, いくら a price.

2.The こそあど grid

Read across for an ending, down for a distance. The two are independent: こ is near the speaker, そ near the listener, あ away from both, and ど asks. Tap a cell to hear it.

A の B is English “B of A”, so the question word comes first: どこの くるま, なんの ほん. The three that want a verb are the three you cannot finish until verbs are taught — say だれとですか and borrow よみますか whole in the meantime.

Four words mean “why”, and they are not interchangeable. なぜ is the formal one and the only one that can never be misread (لِأَيِّ سَبَبٍ). どうして is the default, and it also means “in what manner” (لِمَاذَا). なんで is casual, and literally asks “by what means” (لِمَ). どうやって is not really one of them: it means only “by what method”, which is why it is the safe one (بِأَيِّ طَرِيقَةٍ).

5.なに or なん

SayBeforeExample
なん
nan		 any counter; です; any word starting with t, d or n なんじ · なんですか · なんの
nanji · nan desu ka · nan no
なに
nani		 a particle, or nothing at all なに？ · なにが · なにを
nani? · nani ga · nani o

t, d, n and ん are all made in the same place in the mouth, so なん is simply the form the tongue prefers. The counter clause is the only part that has to be held as a rule — なんかい takes なん despite the k.

6.The time series: a point, and a stretch

Japanese dates run year → month → day, so the top-to-bottom order here is also the order the words appear in a real sentence. The two columns are the other axis: the left asks which one, the right asks how long, and かん (間, “the gap between”) is what moves you across. Arabic draws the same line by swapping أَيُّ for كَمْ.

One counter in the set has nothing to do with time: なんにん nannin, “how many people” (كَمْ شَخْصًا). The frame does not care what you put in it.

7.The frame that carries all of them

〜は 〜ですか。
