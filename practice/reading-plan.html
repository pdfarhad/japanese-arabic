The Reading Plan

Reading speed is not knowledge, it is fitness — built the way fitness is built: many short sessions on material that feels easy. This page is the whole plan in one place: your own reading chapter first, then Tadoku's 76 free graded readers in level order, each one a link away and ticked off here as you finish it. Ten minutes a day; nothing else to decide.

1.The rules the plan runs on

One idea carries everything below: fluency comes from volume and repetition on easy material, never from struggling through hard material. Struggling is study — useful, but a different activity. If a page feels like work, it is the wrong page; go down a level, not up. The Tadoku organisation, whose books fill most of this plan, states it as four golden rules:

Tadoku's four golden rules

  1. Start from scratch — choose easy books you can enjoy without translating.
  2. Don't use a dictionary. Looking words up slows you down and kills the joy of reading.
  3. Skip difficult words, phrases and passages. If the pictures don't help, skip without hesitating.
  4. When the going gets tough, quit the book and pick another. A tough book is the wrong book, not a failing reader.

Two house rules on top, from this course:

  • Romaji off. Open the kana panel (the tab on the left) and switch the romaji layer off before you read. While Latin letters are visible your eye reads them and the kana never get their practice.
  • Read aloud, against the audio. Most books below carry a read-aloud recording on their page. Listen to a page, read it aloud imitating what you heard, then check yourself. Speed lives in the mouth and the ear as much as the eye.

Daily matters more than long: ten minutes every day beats an hour on Sunday, because each re-meeting of a word arrives just as the last one is fading. Missing a day is fine; missing a week starts you half a rung lower, which is also fine — the ladder does not care how often you climb it.

3.The ladder

Step 1 — your own 93 lines

Before the books: Reading 1, the course's own chapter, re-read daily until fluent. It is the only material anywhere that is written exactly from words you have been taught, so it is the easiest text you will ever meet — which by the rules above makes it the right place to build speed first. A line is done when you can say it at the audio's pace without stumbling; the chapter is done when that is true of every line. Expect five or six visits, and expect the last ones to feel too easy. That feeling is the point.

4.When you're stuck

  • Your teacher first. A line that stays hard after the audio and the pictures is a question for the next class — that is what a taught course is for, and the teacher knows which grammar is coming next week anyway.
  • r/LearnJapanese for everything a class cannot answer — how other learners got through this exact stage. Quick questions go in the pinned Daily Thread rather than a new post; the community wiki is the map of everything else, including its own section on reading practice. Search the sub before posting — this stage has been walked many times, and the answers are already good.
